## The role

As Secretary of the Youth Union for the Faculty of Information Systems at UIT, I planned and ran programs, workshops and events at university level, reaching more than **1,500 students**.

In practice that meant: proposing the program, getting it approved, securing budget and venue, recruiting and coordinating volunteers, running the event itself, and reporting on it afterwards.

## What it actually taught me

**A plan is only as good as the person you assigned each step to.** An unowned task is not a task. This is the single most transferable lesson, and it is exactly what I now do with a delivery plan at work.

**Volunteers teach you real management.** Nobody in a student organisation can be compelled to do anything. If people do not understand why the work matters, they simply do not show up. Learning to make the purpose clear — rather than the instruction clear — is a skill I have never stopped using.

**Logistics is a discipline.** Rooms get double-booked. Equipment fails. Someone drops out the night before. The people who run good events are not the ones with the best ideas; they are the ones who thought about what happens when a step fails.

**Speaking to a room.** Two hundred people, a microphone, and a program that has to start on time. Presentation stopped being frightening somewhere in the middle of those three years, which is why walking into a client workshop now does not feel like a big deal.

## Why it is on a technical portfolio

Because this is where my leadership came from, not from work. When I was asked to lead a four-person data team at FPT IS in 2025, the mechanics — set the goal, assign owners, make progress visible, unblock people — were already familiar. I had been practising them since 2021, with a harder crowd.
