## What it covers

- **Data modelling in SAC** — models, dimensions, measures, hierarchies, currency handling.
- **Stories and visualisation** — building analytic content that answers a question rather than displaying a table.
- **Connectivity** — live and import connections to SAP and non-SAP sources.
- **Planning** — SAC's planning capability, versions, data actions.
- **Administration** — users, roles, content lifecycle.

## Why both certifications in the same week

C_ABAPD on 16 April, C_SAC on 17 April 2026 — deliberately back to back, because they are the two halves of the same argument I have been making about my own career.

The ABAP certification says I can build what happens inside the system. The analytics certification says I can carry that data through to the point where somebody makes a decision with it. Most people in the SAP ecosystem specialise in one side. The value I try to offer is that a requirement can be handed to one person and come back as a working end-to-end answer.

## What it added to the warehouse work

My data experience before this was Microsoft-shaped: SQL Server, SSIS, SSAS, Power BI. SAC is the SAP-native answer to the same problem, and knowing both means I can give a client an honest recommendation rather than the one I happen to know.

The short version of that recommendation: **SAC when the data and the audience are both inside the SAP landscape**, particularly with live connections to S/4HANA where you avoid replicating data at all. **Power BI when the reporting spans well beyond SAP** and the organisation already lives in the Microsoft stack.

The planning capability was the part I knew least about going in, and the part I found most interesting — it is the one thing in the analytics space that writes back rather than only reading.
